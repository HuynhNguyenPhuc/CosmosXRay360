## 1. Executive Summary & Paradigm

XraySyn belongs to the **reconstruction-based paradigm (2D → 3D → 2D)**, and is presented in the
paper as, to the authors' knowledge, the first work on single-radiograph novel view synthesis. It
solves the ill-posed 2D→3D transformation in two stages, trained on different data:

1. **3D PriorNet (3DPN)** — learns the radiograph-to-CT transformation *under simulation*, where
   paired CT/DRR data is abundant (LIDC-IDRI CTs).
2. **2D RefineNet (2DRN)** — transfers that learned prior to *real* radiographs (TBX11K), which have
   no paired CT ground truth, refining 3DPN's coarse output.

Both stages are built on a shared differentiable primitive, **CT2Xray**, which the paper positions
as its central contribution: a differentiable forward/backward DRR projector (a differentiable
reimplementation of DeepDRR, Unberath et al. 2018) that lets gradients flow from a synthesized
radiograph back to the 3D CT volume — something DeepDRR's own DRR generator cannot do.

## 2. Mathematical Formulation & Architecture

### A. Differentiable Forward Projector (FP) and Single-Image Backprojector (BP)

Given a CT volume $V_{\text{CT}}$ and homogeneous view matrix $T$, the forward projector integrates
density along each ray (Eq. 1):
$$I(x) = \text{FP}(V_{\text{CT}}, T) = \int V_{\text{CT}}(T^{-1}p)\,d\ell(x) \approx \sum_{p \in \ell(x)} V_{\text{CT}}(T^{-1}p)\Delta p$$
with gradients w.r.t. $V_{\text{CT}}$ defined piecewise per voxel (Eq. 2) so the whole operator is
usable inside a training loop — this is CT2Xray's differentiability contribution over ordinary DRR
simulators.

The **single-image backprojector (BP)**, an *inverse* of FP, spreads a 2D radiograph's intensity
back along each ray into a 3D volume (Eq. 7):
$$V_{\text{BP}}^T(y) = \text{BP}(I(x), T) = \begin{cases} \frac{I(x)}{|\ell(x)|\Delta p} & \text{if } Ty \in \ell(x) \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
This is what turns a single 2D input into a 3D-shaped tensor a 3D UNet can consume.

### B. CT2Xray — material-decomposed attenuation simulation

CT2Xray restricts attenuation to two materials, $m \in \{\text{bone}, \text{tissue}\}$, using a bone
mask $V_{\text{mask}}$ to split material thickness per ray (Eq. 4):
$$t_{\text{bone}}^{\text{CT}}(T) = \text{FP}(V_{\text{CT}} \odot V_{\text{mask}}, T), \quad t_{\text{tissue}}^{\text{CT}}(T) = \text{FP}(V_{\text{CT}} \odot (1 - V_{\text{mask}}), T)$$
and computes the simulated radiograph via Beer-Lambert attenuation over known per-energy material
coefficients $\mu(m, E)$ (Eq. 3, 5, 6):
$$\text{CT2Xray}(V_{\text{CT}}, V_{\text{mask}}, T) = \max(I_{\text{atten}}^T) - I_{\text{atten}}^T, \quad I_{\text{atten}}^T = \sum_E e^{-\sum_m \mu(m,E)\, t_m^{\text{CT}}(T)}$$

### C. 3D PriorNet (3DPN)

3DPN estimates $\{V_{\text{CT}}, V_{\text{mask}}\} = G(\text{BP}(I_{\text{in}}^T, T); \theta)$ with a
3D UNet ($G$, following Çiçek et al. 2016), trained under simulation with a composite loss (Eq. 9-10)
combining an $L_1$ volume-reconstruction term, a cross-entropy bone-mask term, and an $L_1$
radiograph-reprojection term (both at the input pose $T$ and a second sampled pose $T'$) so the
recovered volume stays consistent under CT2Xray reprojection, not just at the input view.

### D. 2D RefineNet (2DRN)

2DRN closes the sim-to-real domain gap and adds back detail 3DPN's coarse, memory-constrained volume
loses. It refines the material-thickness maps $t_m^{\text{CT}}$ with a Residual Dense Network (RDN,
Zhang et al. 2018) whose conv-filter parameters are partly generated by a second network $\mathcal{M}$
fed the *original* input radiograph directly (Eq. 11) — intended to recover information lost during
3DPN's lossy 2D→3D→2D round trip. The refined thicknesses feed back through an augmented CT2Xray (Eq.
12) to produce the final synthesized radiograph $I_{\text{final}}^{T'}$, trained with an $L_1$
reconstruction term plus a **Least-Squares GAN (LSGAN)** adversarial term against a PatchGAN-style
discriminator $D$ (Eq. 13-14).

## 3. Reported Training & Evaluation Setup

- **3DPN dataset:** LIDC-IDRI (1,018 CT volumes; volumes with >2.5mm between-slice resolution
  discarded → 780 usable; 700 train / 10 val / 70 test).
- **2DRN dataset:** TBX11K real radiographs (3,000 hand-selected healthy-category images to avoid
  interfering foreign objects/abnormal anatomy; 2,600 train / 100 val / 300 test), cropped/resized to
  256×256.
- **3DPN training view sampling:** $T$, $T'$ sampled randomly in azimuth and elevation over
  **[-18°, 18°]**.
- **2DRN training/testing view range:** for real radiographs, $T$ is assumed canonical frontal (no
  ground-truth pose available for real data); $T'$ is sampled over a **±9° azimuth** neighborhood —
  twenty view angles uniformly spaced from -9° to 9° at test time. **The paper never evaluates a full
  360° sweep** — this is the narrow validated range this project's `docs/LOSSES.md`/`LOG.md`
  training-scope fix widens for the full-360° benchmark.
- **Hardware/compute:** PyTorch, 4× NVIDIA P6000 GPUs, ~5 days training.
- Internal volume/working resolution 128×128×128, radiographs resized to 256×256 for 2DRN.

### Reported results (Table 1, abridged)

- Simulated (PSNR/SSIM, $I_{\text{CT}}^T$ input-view reconstruction): 3DPN **29.49/0.961** vs.
  ablations 2D Refiner 21.09/0.801, X2CT 20.44/0.755, 3DPN-DRR 21.05/0.925 — 3DPN's full material-
  decomposed CT2Xray beats all ablated variants by 6-7dB PSNR.
- Real radiographs (novel view, PSNR/SSIM + FID since no ground truth): 3DPN 22.75/0.819 (FID 1.090)
  → XraySyn$^{\text{no}\mathcal{M}}$ 28.42/0.857 (FID 0.375) → full XraySyn (with $\mathcal{M}$)
  **30.33/0.865 (FID 0.319)** — each added component measurably improves both fidelity and realism.

## 4. Stated Limitations

1. **Resolution limited by 3DPN's memory constraint.** The paper's conclusion states this directly:
   3DPN's 3D UNet over a full volume is memory-bound, capping achievable resolution; the authors
   propose a "more efficient network design" as future work, not something the paper itself solves.
2. **No ground-truth pose for real radiographs.** Since TBX11K has no paired CT, the source pose $T$
   for real inputs must be *assumed* canonical frontal — an architectural constraint the paper's own
   2DRN training/testing never relaxes, matching this project's finding (`docs/LOSSES.md`) that
   `net3d`/`backproj` are frozen and hardwired to the frontal source pose.
3. **Narrow validated angular range.** Real-radiograph results are only reported for ±9° (twenty
   views); the paper does not claim or demonstrate fidelity at the full 360° range this project
   evaluates against.
4. **Domain gap between simulated and real training signal.** 2DRN is trained without direct 3D
   supervision on real radiographs (no paired CT), relying on the LSGAN + $L_1$ terms and the frozen
   3DPN prior; the paper notes some residual background noise leaks through via the residual
   connection despite $\mathcal{M}$'s attempt to constrict information flow.
